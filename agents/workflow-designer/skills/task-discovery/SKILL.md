# Task discovery

Goal: a short summary of the task that the person reads and says "yes,
that's it". No design until then.

## Ask, one or two at a time

1. **Goal**: what are you trying to get done, and for whom?
2. **Trigger**: what starts it (a message, a date, a request, a problem)?
3. **Walk-through**: take me through the last time you did it, step by step.
   What did you open, read, decide, write, send?
4. **Decisions**: where did you have to judge something? What did you look
   at to decide?
5. **Pain**: which step is slow, error-prone, boring, or easy to forget?
6. **Done**: how do you know it is finished, and who else needs to know?
7. **Frequency and volume**: how often, and how many at a time?
8. **Variations**: what changes from one time to the next? What is the odd
   case that breaks the usual way?

Ask for a real recent example, not the ideal. If the person can show the
thing itself (a document, a thread, a screen), look at it.

## The discovery summary

Write it back in their words:

```markdown
## <Task name>
Goal: <one sentence>
Trigger: <what starts it>  ·  How often: <frequency, volume>
Steps today:
1. <step> (<who/what tool>)  <- pain: <if any>
...
Decisions: <the judgments, and what they are based on>
Done when: <outcome, and who is told>
Variations: <the odd cases>
```

Get an explicit confirmation or corrections. Record the confirmed summary
(the `memory` skill) before moving on to `workflow-design`.
