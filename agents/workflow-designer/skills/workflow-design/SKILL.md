# Workflow design

Input: a confirmed discovery summary. Output: a workflow the person agrees
to, ready for `design-handoff`.

## Principles

- **The person decides; the agent prepares.** The agent gathers, drafts,
  checks and proposes; the person reviews, chooses and approves.
- **One decision per step.** Each screen asks the person for one thing.
- **Fewer steps than today.** If the new flow is not clearly shorter or
  safer, rethink it.
- **Visible progress.** The person always sees where they are, what is done
  and what is next.
- **Interruptible.** Work can stop at any step and resume later where it was.
- **Not a form.** If a step is "fill in these fields", ask what decision the
  fields serve, and have the agent fill them from what it knows, with the
  person confirming.

## The step table

| # | Step | Person | Agent | System | Person sees |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | <name> | <decides/approves what> | <prepares what> | <records/sends what> | <the one thing on screen> |

Mark the decision points and the approvals (anything that sends, spends,
deletes or commits needs the person's yes).

## Screen sketches

For each step, a rough text sketch: the heading, the one question or
choice, what the agent shows to support it, and the actions. Keep it to a
few lines; the design team does the visual design.

## Check before handoff

- Every pain point from discovery is addressed, or deliberately left, with
  the reason.
- Every variation has a path (even "hand back to the person").
- The person walked through it and agreed.
